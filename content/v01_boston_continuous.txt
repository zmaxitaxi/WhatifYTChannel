VISUAL AND AUDIO DIRECTION
One connected riverbank, campus, brick workshop, research quay, ferry route, transit neighborhood, clean-energy mill, and future harbor. The camera curves through the world, travels with the ferry, and rises into a sunlit final reveal. There are no cuts or hidden scene swaps. People walk, work, carry instruments, build, travel, and board the ferry. Different people represent successive generations; nobody is depicted living from 1885 to 2050.
Plain centered white serif captions without boxes. A persistent checkpoint timeline and smoothly moving red dot remain readable throughout. Lighting and color become warmer/brighter as the story opens toward its optimistic future.
An original continuous 72-second musical score develops from intimate warm keys into a gently uplifting harbor theme. Eight quiet effects mark meaningful events; no bird/chirp/wind beds.

REAL-WORLD HISTORICAL ANCHORS
Stanford was founded in California in 1885 and opened in 1891. https://www.stanford.edu/about/history/
Hewlett-Packard was founded in 1939 after its founders worked in a Palo Alto garage. https://www.hp.com/hpinfo/abouthp/histnfacts/garage/brief.pdf
Stanford Industrial Park was authorized in 1951; its first occupant arrived in 1953. https://www-leland.stanford.edu/group/OTL/documents/JSstanfordpark.pdf

All sources rechecked October 31, 2026. Boston was already an innovation center in real history; this film imagines another path, not a claim that Stanford alone created Silicon Valley or that its relocation prevents unrelated historical events.
